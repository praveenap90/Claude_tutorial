Lesson 1

Meet Claude

Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic. You write to it in plain language, attach files if you want, and it answers in conversation. It can draft, explain, analyze, plan, and write code with you.

Good at

Thinking and writing

  • Drafting and editing text in any tone
  • Explaining hard ideas at your level
  • Reading long documents and pulling out what matters
  • Writing, explaining, and fixing code
Check its work

Facts that matter

  • Exact figures, dates, quotes, and citations
  • Anything recent, unless web search is on
  • Legal, medical, and money decisions: use it to prepare, then confirm with a professional
By default

What it won't do on its own

  • Look at the web unless search is on
  • Carry memory between chats unless memory is on
  • Touch your apps unless you connect them

    Lesson 2

    Prompts that get good answers

    A prompt is whatever you type. Claude can only work with what you give it, so the biggest improvement you can make is telling it more about what you want.

    Goal
    What you want at the end: an email, a plan, a decision, an explanation.
    Context
    The situation and the facts Claude can't see. Who, what, when, what you already tried.
    Audience
    Who will read or use the result. A manager, a ten-year-old, yourself next week.
    Format and length
    Bullets, a table, two paragraphs, 150 words, three options.
    Example
    A sample of the style or structure you like. One good example beats a paragraph of description.
    Vague

    Write about dogs.

    Specific

    Write a 150-word intro for a newsletter aimed at first-time dog owners. Friendly tone, one practical tip about the first week at home, and end with a question readers can reply to.

    Prompt Lab

    Switch pieces on and watch the prompt grow
    Your prompt

    What Claude might write

    Example only, to show the difference. Real replies vary.

    Follow up, don't start over

    Treat the first answer as a draft. Short follow-ups work well:

    • "Make it half as long."
    • "More formal, and drop the second paragraph."
    • "Give me three different versions."
    • "What did you assume that I didn't tell you?"

    Let Claude ask you

    For big or fuzzy tasks, invite questions first.

    I want to plan a career change. Ask me up to five questions, one at a time, before you suggest anything.

    Mark off your materials

    When you paste text or data, wrap it in simple tags so Claude knows where your instructions end and your material begins.

    Summarize the text below in 3 bullets. <text> ...your text here... </text>

    More on this in Anthropic's prompt engineering guide.

    Lesson 3

    Tokens and the context window

    Claude reads and writes in small chunks of text called tokens, and it can only keep so much in view at once. That limit is the context window. Knowing both explains why long chats drift and why big files change how a chat behaves.

    What a token is

    A chunk of text: often a short word, or part of a longer one. In English, a token is roughly four characters, and 100 tokens is about 75 words. Code, numbers, and other languages can split into more tokens.

    Input and output

    Input tokens are what you send: your messages, files, and instructions. Output tokens are what Claude writes back. Developers using the API are billed for the two separately.

    The context window

    Everything Claude can consider at one time: your messages, attached files, its own replies, search results, and standing instructions. It is Claude working memory for the current chat.

    What to do when it gets full

    What you noticeWhy it happensTry this
    Claude loses early details in a long chatThe chat holds a lot, and long conversations can outgrow what Claude keeps in view.Restate the key facts, or ask for a summary and continue in a new chat.
    A very large file makes answers shallowThe file takes up much of the window.Ask about specific sections, or split the file into parts.
    A long answer stops partwayReplies have a length limit.Say "continue from where you stopped", or ask for one section at a time.
    You change topics after many messagesOld material still sits in the window and can distract.Start a new chat for the new topic.
    You hit usage limits sooner than expectedLonger chats and bigger files generally use more of your allowance.Keep chats focused and attach only what the task needs.

    How an app handles a very long conversation depends on the product, so do not count on every early detail being remembered perfectly.

    Carry a long chat into a new one

    Summarize the key decisions, facts, constraints, and open questions from this chat in under 300 words, so I can paste it into a new chat and continue.

    Keep answers lean

    Answer in under 100 words. Lead with the recommendation, then give the two most important reasons.

    Lesson 5

    Files, images, and Projects

    Claude works best on your real material. Attach it to a message, or keep it in a Project so every related chat starts with the same background.

    What you can attach

    • Documents and PDFs
    • Spreadsheets and CSV files
    • Images, screenshots, and photos
    • Text and code files

    What to ask for

    • Summaries and key points
    • Questions about specific sections
    • Comparisons between two files
    • A description or critique of an image

    Good habits

    • Say what you want from the file
    • Point to pages or sections
    • Ask Claude to quote or cite where it found something, then check
    Try this with any PDF

    Read the attached document. Give me a 5-bullet summary, then list any numbers or dates I should double-check against the original. Tell me which page each one came from.

    Try this with a screenshot or photo

    Here's a screenshot of an error message I keep seeing. Explain in plain language what it means and give me the three most likely fixes, starting with the easiest.

    Projects, by example

    A Project is a workspace for one ongoing effort

    A Project holds files and instructions that every chat inside it can use. You write the background once instead of repeating it. Good fits: a class, a job search, a client, a home renovation.

    ProjectSpring semester: Biology 201
    Project instructions

    I'm a first-year student. Explain things step by step in plain language. When I ask a practice question, wait for my answer before showing the solution. Keep answers under 200 words unless I ask for more.

    syllabus.pdflecture-notes-ch4.pdfexam-1-practice.pdf

    Why it works: every new chat in this Project already knows the course, the files, and how you like to learn.

    Lesson 6

    Make things, not just answers

    Ask for a finished piece of work and Claude can produce it as a document, slide deck, spreadsheet, design, chart, or a small working tool. These appear beside the chat as artifacts you can keep, change, and share.

    Request builder

    Pick an output, then replace the [brackets]
    Template

    Edit by talking

    You don't have to start over. Say "make the title shorter", "add a column for deadlines", or "change the colors to something calmer".

    Ask for the format you need

    If you need a particular file type such as a Word or PowerPoint file, say so in the request. Many artifacts can also be downloaded or exported.

    Start rough

    Get a first version quickly, react to it, then refine. It's faster than describing everything up front.

    Lesson 7

    Search and research

    Claude's built-in knowledge stops at a training cutoff. Web search lets it look things up now. Research goes further: it runs many searches and writes a longer report.

    Turn search on for
    • News and recent events
    • Prices, versions, schedules, rules
    • Who currently holds a role
    • Anything with "latest", "current", or "still"
    Search isn't needed for
    • Stable facts and definitions
    • Explaining a concept
    • Editing or brainstorming from your own text
    Research mode

    Use it when you want a thorough comparison or report with sources and can wait a few minutes. Availability depends on your plan and settings.

    Search or no search?

    0 of 5
    Try this with search on

    Compare the three most popular note-taking apps right now. Give me a table of price, platforms, and standout feature. Link each claim to its source and tell me where sources disagree.

    Always check: open the sources for anything you'll rely on. Ask Claude "which parts of this are you least sure about?"

    Lesson 8

    Make Claude yours

    Memory and preferences let Claude know your context so you repeat yourself less. Both are controlled in Settings, and what is available depends on your plan.

    Memory

    Claude can carry useful details from your chats into later ones, such as your role or ongoing projects. You control it in Settings, and you can ask Claude to remember or forget something.

    Remember that I prefer short answers with the recommendation first.

    Preferences

    Tell Claude how you like it to respond: tone, level of detail, formatting. They apply across chats.

    Explain things plainly. Skip long introductions. Use bullets only when I ask.

    Connectors, skills, and plugins

    These extend what Claude can reach and how it works. They have their own lessons, so this page stays short.

    Want a clean slate?Use an incognito chat, or turn off memory generation in Settings, and nothing is carried over from earlier chats.
    Lesson 9

    Plugins, connectors, skills, and MCP

    Four words that show up together. Connectors give Claude access to your tools. Skills teach it how you do a task. Plugins package both into one install. MCP is the standard underneath connectors.

    How the pieces fit

    SkillYour steps, format, or checklist

    A skill gives Claude instructions it reads when your request needs them. A connector lets Claude read from and act in a service. A plugin is an optional wrapper that ships both, and sometimes more, in one install.

    Go deeper: where plugins work, and rules for teams
    Where they work

    Saved to your account

    Plugins, skills, and connectors you add in the web app or desktop app follow you to chat on web, desktop, and mobile, and to Claude Code when you sign in with the same account. Things added from the Claude Code command line stay on that computer.

    Where they work

    Chat versus longer tasks

    In chat, a plugin brings its skills, commands, and connectors. Longer task sessions in the desktop app and Claude Code also run its agents and hooks.

    Teams

    Owners set the rules

    On Team and Enterprise plans, Owners decide what members can add and share. Admins can require certain plugins and, on Enterprise, restrict which plugins and local MCP servers are allowed.

    MCP in plain words

    What a connector really is

    A service runs an MCP server that offers a list of tools, such as search, read, or create. A connector is Claude linked to that server. You sign in with the service, and your permission settings decide which tools Claude may use. Most people never touch MCP directly. You meet it when you add a custom connector by its server address, or when a developer builds one for a team tool.

    Lesson 10

    Skills: teach Claude your way of doing things

    A skill is a short set of written instructions for one kind of task, such as your weekly report or your meeting notes. Claude uses it when a task calls for it, so you stop re-explaining the same steps.

    Skill, instructions, or memory?

    Pick the right tool
    ToolBest forExample
    A promptOne-off requests"Summarize this article in 3 bullets."
    Project instructionsBackground for one body of work"This project is our wedding. Budget is fixed."
    Preferences and memoryFacts about you, across all chats"Keep answers short, recommendation first."
    A skillA repeatable procedure with steps and a set output"Every Friday report: these sections, this order, this tone."

    Would a skill help?

    Tap a situation

    Try it: draft a skill

    Fill in the boxes, then copy the draft
    Skill draft

    Paste the draft into a chat and ask Claude to polish it, then add it under Customize, then Skills. Menu names and who can add skills depend on your plan, so check the help center if yours looks different.

    Worked example: a personal LinkedIn post from something you read

    Using a LinkedIn writing skill
    1. Find a skill. Open Customize, then Skills, and browse the skill directory for a LinkedIn or social-post writing skill. Check who published it before you add it. Directories and plan options differ, so if you cannot find one, use the Skill builder above to write your own.
    2. Turn it on and start a new chat. Claude will use the skill when you ask for a LinkedIn post.
    3. Bring your source. Paste your own notes or a short summary of the book, blog post, or news story. For a book, use your highlights, not whole chapters.
    4. Add the personal part. This is what makes the post sound like you: where you saw the idea in your own work, or what changed your mind.
    5. Ask for a draft, then edit it in your own voice. You review it and post it yourself.

    Try it. Pick what you read, then fill in the boxes.

    Ask Claude

    What a good draft sounds likeA short opening line that makes someone stop scrolling. Then the idea in one or two sentences, your own moment ("last month I..."), one takeaway, and a closing question. About 120 to 200 words, with no buzzwords.
    Keep it honest

    Do not let Claude invent your story

    Give it a true detail, even a small one. If you leave it out, the post turns generic. Check any quote or number against the source.

    Good habit

    Name the source

    Credit the author or link the article. Summarize in your own words rather than copying passages.

    File skills

    Claude already has skills for making Word documents, PowerPoint decks, Excel sheets, and PDFs, which is why it can hand you finished files.

    Tips for good skills

    • One skill, one job
    • Say when it should be used
    • Describe the finished result, not only the steps
    • Test it on a small example first

    Lesson 11

    Connect Gmail, GitHub, Slack, and more

    Connectors let Claude read from the apps where your work lives and, with your approval, act in them. Instead of pasting an email thread into the chat, you connect Gmail once and ask for a summary of the thread about the venue contract.

    Add a connector, step by step

    Same flow for most services
    1. Open Customize, then Connectors. In the desktop app, choose Customize in the sidebar. Menu names can change, so search the help center if yours looks different.
    2. Find the service. Browse the directory or search by name, such as Gmail, GitHub, or Slack. Check whether a listing is marked Verified or Community.
    3. Click to connect. Some services first ask for a server address or your account region. On Team or Enterprise plans, an Owner may need to add the connector for your organization first.
    4. Sign in and approve. You log in on the service's own page and approve the access it lists. Claude never sees your password.
    5. Check the status. The connector should show as Connected.
    6. Turn it on in a chat. Click the + in the message box, choose Connectors, and switch it on for this conversation. Then ask for something that needs the app.
    7. Set permissions. On the connector's page, set each group of tools to Always allow, Needs approval, or Blocked.

    Source: Anthropic's help pages on getting started with connectors and custom connectors.

    Morning prep

    Check my calendar for tomorrow. For each meeting, find the most recent related email thread and give me a two-line summary and one question I should be ready to answer.

    Rule of thumb: let reading run, keep anything that sends, posts, or changes on Needs approval, and block what you never want. Lesson 9 has a table of example tools with suggested settings.

    Treat what Claude reads as dataAn email, web page, or document can contain text that tries to give Claude orders. If Claude points out an instruction inside such content, or asks to do something you did not request, decline.

    When something goes wrong

    ProblemWhat to try
    Can't find the serviceSearch by name. On a work plan, ask your Owner to add it. If the tool offers a connection address (called a remote MCP server), you can add it as a custom connector.
    Connected, but Claude doesn't use itOpen the + menu in the chat, choose Connectors, and switch it on for this conversation.
    It shows ReconnectAccess lapsed. Click Reconnect and sign in again.
    You want Claude to stop using itTurning off the chat toggle only pauses it. Use Disconnect to revoke access, or Remove to delete the connector.
    Claude keeps asking for approvalSet that group of tools to Always allow if it only reads. Keep sending and changing tools on Needs approval.

    Custom connectors are in beta and work with servers you enter by address. Only add servers you trust, and review which tools you allow.

      Lesson 12

      Organize files and automate tasks

      With the Claude desktop app, Claude can work on folders on your computer: sort and rename files, build an index, write a report. It can also repeat a job for you on a schedule. What is available depends on your plan and settings.

      How a folder job runs

      You decide what Claude can reach
      1. Open the chat in the desktop app on the computer that has the files.
      2. Connect a folder. Claude can only see the folders you choose.
      3. Ask for a plan first. Have Claude list what it would change before it changes anything.
      4. Approve, then let it work. Look over the result when it finishes.
      Built-in care

      New files first

      Claude saves results next to your originals and changes an existing file only when you ask it to.

      Built-in care

      Deleting needs your OK

      Claude asks before it deletes anything. If you would rather not allow that, it moves files into a _to_delete folder for you to review and empty yourself.

      Good to know

      Your computer must be reachable

      If the computer is asleep, offline, or the app is closed, Claude can't reach the folder. You can attach files to the chat instead.

      Lesson 15
      
          
      Using Claude well
      
          
      A few habits separate people who get steady value from people who get stuck.
      
        
      
        
      
          
      If this happensTry this
      
          
            
      The answer is genericAdd context, audience, and a format. Use the Prompt Lab pieces from lesson 2.
      
            
      The answer is too longSet a length: "under 100 words" or "3 bullets".
      
            
      Claude misunderstood youSay what was wrong, then restate the goal. Or ask "what did you assume?"
      
            
      You doubt a factAsk for sources, turn on search, and check the original.
      
            
      A long chat gets muddled, or you change topicsSummarize and start a new chat. Lesson 3 explains why.
      
          
      
        
      
          
      Verify what matters

      The thumbs up and thumbs down buttons on a response tell Anthropic what worked and what didn't.
